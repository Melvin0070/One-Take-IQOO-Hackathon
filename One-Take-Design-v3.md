## Read This First

**One-Take is a script supervisor in the viewfinder.** While you film, it knows which lines of your script still lack a clean take, asks for exactly those between lines, tells you when it's safe to wrap, and plays the finished, captioned cut the moment you stop. On the phone, offline.

**What v3 changes.** It folds in the Sep 10 review notes and new research, and adds what v2 deferred: the product experience, a developer surface, trust (privacy, security, evaluation), market and business, and the demo. It turns the reviewers' loopholes into requirements with acceptance tests. **It does not make engineering decisions.** Where v2 named a library, clock or protocol, v3 keeps it as the current proposal and lists what eng review must settle.

## 1. Summary

### The pitch in three lines

> Descript deletes your bad take. One-Take makes sure you have a good one before you leave the set.
>
> A script supervisor in the viewfinder, offline.
>
> Nothing left this phone. (Said on stage as "nothing left this phone but the picture on that screen" whenever we're mirroring — §11.)

### What's new in v3

- **Safe to wrap.** The record button becomes Wrap when every line has a clean take. It's the payoff of coverage, and a cold jury reads it in two seconds.
- **Lined script, and The Vanish.** Every line shows covered, needed or pending with scratched takes struck through; re-say a line, or say "scratch that", and the flub is gone from the cut that plays on stop. Every flag gives its reason in words.
- **Must-say lines.** Mark a disclosure, a qualification or a learning objective as must-say; it needs a word-perfect take before the wrap report calls it covered. It's the mechanism behind the wedge: brief mode for finance and health creators and the agencies that brief them (§8).
- **Trust, made checkable.** No network calls in the single-phone path, a threat model, delete on stage, and an eval card measured on **held-out** stranger reads with sample sizes stated honestly.
- **NPU in every session, shown live.** A vision model runs on the NPU in every session and does a real job in the edit, with an engine-stats overlay for the stage, so the hardware story doesn't hang on NPU speech recognition.

Every addition, with what it costs and which tier it lands in, is §3.4; the change log against v2 is Appendix C. **Demoted for Chennai:** Hinglish and Tanglish script lines, caption presets, cutaway prompts and placement guidance move to the Finale backlog.

## 2. Problem and Users

### Problem

Talking creators (educators, reviewers, finfluencers, founders, coaches; the ₹70k flagship-phone owner) lose the gap between "filmed" and "posted" to an editing session: cutting silences and flubs, captioning, reframing, picking the best of several takes. Post-hoc tools now automate most of that cleanup. None of them can fix a take that was never recorded: the creator finds out at the desk that line 3 has no clean read, after the light, the setup and the energy are gone.

One-Take moves that check to the moment of capture.

### Who it's for

| Segment | Situation | Why One-Take |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary (Chennai build):** solo scripted creator, one phone, no crew | Lessons, product explainers, paid integrations, founder updates; reads from a script or near-script | Every line covered before they pack up; the cut is ready on set |
| **Approver (the wedge, §8):** the agency or brand manager who briefs finance and health creators; later course producers and comms leads | Needs required lines and disclosures said correctly; wants fewer revision rounds | Must-say lines, the wrap report, brief mode |
| **Integrator (Finale and beyond):** OEM camera team | Wants creator features in the stock camera | The Coverage Engine (§6) |

**Not for:** trend and template creators (conceded to CapCut), live streaming, long unscripted vlogs. Free-talk mode (Tier 1) helps them, but it isn't the spine.

### Jobs to be done

1. "Before I pack up, tell me I have every line."
2. "Show me the cut now, so I can judge it while I can still reshoot."
3. "Don't make me scrub through takes."
4. "Don't send my face anywhere."

## 3. Why It Wins

### 3.1 The claim: coverage, live

Live speech in capture tools does three things today, and none of them judges a line (about 25 products checked Sep 10; Appendix B):

| What exists | Who | When | What it can't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice-following teleprompters: scroll with you, pause when you go off-script, obey "restart" commands | Pixel 11 Creator Suite (stock camera), PromptSmart, Teleprompter.com, VoicePrompter, ScriptFox, Elgato, Speakflow, Captions | Live | Judge whether a line was delivered cleanly, ask for a retake, track coverage |
| Fixed-speed teleprompters | vivo's camera app (since the X80 Pro in 2022; still on the X300 Ultra with OriginOS 6), Instagram's camera and Edits, CapCut, Descript | Live | Anything involving speech |
| Retake removal and script-aware timelines | Descript, Gling, DaVinci Resolve IntelliScript, ScriptFox Studio, Avid ScriptSync | After recording, on a desktop | Create a take that was never recorded |
| Live reading-error detection | Amira ("delivers intervention in the moment"), Google Read Along, Microsoft Reading Coach | Live, for literacy | Video, takes, a cut |
| Script supervision on film sets | ScriptE and similar: lined scripts and circled takes, by hand | Live, manual | Listen |

**What's new is the combination, inside the camera, during the take:** a per-line clean-or-flubbed judgement, a prompt to redo that line, a live coverage state, a Safe to wrap signal, and an automatic cut of the clean takes on stop. No product we found says coverage is complete, and none lets a spoken phrase mark a take (both negatives are from searches).

**The claim, as we say it:** "As of Sep 10, 2026 we found no camera or teleprompter app that checks every scripted line as you film, asks you to redo the ones you skipped or flubbed, and cuts the clean takes together the moment you stop. Voice-following prompters only scroll or pause. Retake cleanup happens after recording. Reading tutors catch misreads live; we bring that into the viewfinder."

**Biggest pitch risk: the host's camera has a teleprompter.** vivo has shipped one since 2022; whether the iQOO 15 has it, and whether it follows your voice, is Open Question 21. One-Take is never pitched as a teleprompter. It's a script supervisor, and for iQOO it's the layer that turns their teleprompter into one (the OEM path, §8).

### 3.2 Moments a cold jury reads in seconds

1. **"Again, line 2"** appears in the pause, never mid-sentence.
2. **Line 2 turns covered** on the lined script, and the flub is struck through.
3. **Safe to wrap:** "All 3 covered", and the record button becomes Wrap.
4. **The Vanish:** the cut plays on stop, and the flub and the "scratch that" are gone.
5. **Trust:** tap the struck take to see why it was flagged; then delete the juror's clip in front of them, with airplane mode visible and the mirror accounted for out loud (§11).

### 3.3 Why on-device is structural, not a preference

- **Timing:** the prompt has to land in the pause between two lines, which is under a second. A cloud round trip on venue or mobile networks can't promise that.
- **Privacy:** creators', students' and employees' faces and voices stay on the phone, so a school or company can use it without adding a third-party processor.
- **Bandwidth:** three phones of 1080p video can't round-trip a venue network (multicam).
- **Cost:** no per-minute inference bill, so the free tier can stay generous.

### 3.5 What we don't claim

- Not the first to remove bad takes: Descript, Gling, DaVinci Resolve and ScriptFox do it after recording.
- Not a teleprompter, and not the first to follow a script by voice: vivo's camera has had a teleprompter since 2022, and Pixel 11, Instagram and a dozen apps ship one.
- Not the first to catch reading errors live: Amira and Google Read Along do it for literacy.
- No Hinglish caption moat (commoditized), no render-time promise, no gaze prompts, no "<100 ms".
- No "no network permission" claim for multicam builds (Android needs INTERNET even for local sockets), and no DPDP compliance claim.

## 4. Premises

Locked Sep 10 in v2; v3 changes are marked.

1. **Coverage is the headline; the edit is the proof.** Post-hoc tools already cut, caption and fix eye contact. Only a live system changes what gets filmed.
2. **Every feature stays, ranked in tiers with gates.** At a missed gate, features drop from the bottom of the ranking up; the spine never drops. Tier 3 is the Finale backlog.
3. **Captions are a full feature, not the moat claim.** Script-mode captions come from the script; free-talk captions get an accuracy pass after stop.
4. **The Director is a script supervisor first, a coach second.** Gaze is a score, not a prompt (EyeGaze's 14.9° mean error).
5. **(Changed Sep 10) Talking points lead Tier 2; multicam follows.** Talking-points mode takes coverage beyond verbatim scripts to creators who work from bullet points. Multicam keeps its sharper job (the edit picks the angle, not whoever is speaking), must be paired and encrypted (R10), and becomes the Bengaluru Finale's headline. It goes on stage only if it passes its check.
6. **The edit plays the moment you stop; export renders in the background.** No render-time promise.
7. **(Changed) Every session uses the NPU, and its signal does a real job.** An on-device vision model runs on the NPU in every session from Tier 0. Under thermal pressure its duty cycle drops but never to zero, so R9 holds in hot-phone mode. Its off-frame signal is used in the edit (§10 step 6), so the chip isn't a prop. Recognition moves to the NPU **per requirement, not globally**: only when it beats its latency bar (p90 verdict latency below the line-end pause on a loaner) *and* R2, R3 and R8 still pass on the held-out corpus with it. ~~If the NPU export returns no word timings, the one-breath split (R3) stays on the CPU recognizer;~~ **REVISED Sep 11: no recognizer supplies usable word boundaries. R3 no longer needs them (it splits coverage, not audio). Separately, AI Hub's Whisper exports are per-chipset QNN context binaries that do not run through LiteRT at all, so "NPU recognition" is a different runtime integration entirely, not a switch.** if it invents text on short clips, commands (R8) stay on keyword spotting. Which models and runtimes: eng review.
8. **(Changed) Chennai demos in English script mode.** Tanglish moves to the Finale backlog with Hinglish; the Bengaluru Finale demos in Hinglish.
9. **Nothing goes on stage unmeasured.** Numbers include precision and recall on strangers' reads, with the device named.
10. **(New) Precision first.** A clean read flagged in front of a juror costs more trust than a missed flub, which the pickup list still catches. Thresholds lean to precision, and every automatic decision is visible and reversible.
11. **(New) Private by construction.** The single-phone product path makes no network calls; nothing leaves app storage without an explicit export; the demo runs in airplane mode.
12. **Kept from Aug 22:** the talker is the user; non-destructive editing (raw files plus an edit list, always); thermal is a design input; the event is the go-to-market (a Play-shippable app and an OEM-preload audition).

## 5. Product Experience

For `/plan-design-review` and `/design-consultation`. This section fixes behavior and hierarchy, not visual style.

### 5.1 Vocabulary

Borrowed from film sets because it's exact and creators already half-know it. Use these words in the UI; never show "similarity", "confidence" or "utterance".

| Word | Meaning in One-Take |
|---|---|
| Script | The pasted lines. Each line has a type: **line** (default), **must-say** (word-perfect), **talking point** (Tier 2), **cutaway** (Tier 3) |
| Take | One attempt at a line |
| Unread | No take yet for this line (the state a line starts in) |
| Covered | The line has a clean take |
| Needed | Flubbed, missed or scratched; still needs a take |
| Pending | The verdict hasn't arrived yet |
| Circle | Choose a take over the automatic pick |
| Scratch | Mark a take unusable, by voice, tap, remote, or automatically |
| Off-frame | The face left the frame during that take: a note on the take, never a flag on the line |
| Video missing | The take's verdict survived a crash but its video didn't; it can't be used in the cut |
| Pickups | A short session that records only the needed lines, into the same project |
| Wrap | Stop, ideally when every line is covered |
| Wrap report | One page of coverage for the project |

Reasons are plain words: "missed 'every Sunday'", "restarted", "said 'seventy' for 'seven'", "extra words".

### 5.2 Primary journey

| # | The creator | One-Take shows | They feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Copies a script on the laptop, taps Paste in a new project (Office Kit clipboard) | Lines split one per row; count and rough read time | "It understood my script" |
| 2 | Marks the disclosure line must-say (Tier 1) | A must-say badge on that line | In control |
| 3 | Puts the phone on a stand and frames up | Preview; face-in-frame chip; heat and battery chip | Ready |
| 4 | Taps Record | The strip under the lens: current line large, next line dim; coverage "0 of 3" | Focused |
| 5 | Reads line 1, pauses | Line 1 ticks covered; the strip advances | Momentum |
| 6 | Stumbles on line 2, finishes, pauses | "Again, line 2" in the pause | Caught, not scolded |
| 7 | Re-reads line 2 | Line 2 turns covered; the first attempt is struck through | Relief |
| 8 | Reads line 3 | "All 3 covered · Safe to wrap"; Record becomes Wrap | Confident: this is the product |
| 9 | Taps Wrap | The captioned cut plays at once | Delight |
| 10 | Glances at the lined script | Each line with the take in the cut; struck takes with reasons; circle, scratch, undo | Trust |
| 11 | If a line is needed: taps "Record 1 pickup" | A pickup session for just that line | "Fixed in 20 seconds" |
| 12 | Taps Export, or Send to laptop | Background export progress; file in the gallery or on the laptop | Done |
| 13 | Taps Delete project (optional) | Raw files, ledger and app-made exports gone | "It's mine" |

**Wrapping early is never blocked.** The wrap sheet says "Line 3 needs a take" with Record pickup and Wrap anyway. For a must-say line, Wrap anyway asks for confirmation, and the wrap report shows that line as not covered.

**Line coverage states.** One state per line, derived from the ledger:

| From | Event | To |
|---|---|---|
| unread | A take for this line closes | pending |
| unread | Speech matches a later line (skipped) | needed |
| pending | Verdict clean (must-say: word-perfect) | covered |
| pending | Verdict flubbed or missing words | needed |
| needed | A new take for this line closes | pending |
| covered | A new take of **that line** closes (the creator re-read a line already covered) | covered; the new take replaces the one in the cut only if it's clean. It is never scored against the current line — see the joining rule in §10 |
| covered | Its only clean take is scratched | needed |
| needed | The scratch is undone | covered |
| any | The line's text is edited after takes exist | needed; the line id is unchanged so pickups still join the cut, and existing takes are kept, marked "read against an earlier version" (R18) |
| any | **The line is deleted** | Its takes are marked orphaned and kept, never erased (Premise 12, non-destructive). New Sep 11, R24 |
| any | **Lines are reordered** | Line ids are unchanged; cut order is re-derived from the new positions. New Sep 11, R24 |
| covered | Its only clean take's video is lost to a crash | needed; the take shows as video missing (R13) |

Past the hold limit the strip moves on, but the line stays pending until its verdict lands — and a pending line blocks Safe to wrap, however long it takes (§5.4 principle 5). Safe to wrap shows only when every line is covered.

### 5.4 UX principles

1. **Eyes on the lens.** During a take everything is glanceable from reading distance, in the strip under the lens. No dialogs while recording.
2. **Never interrupt speech.** Prompts appear only in the pause after a line.
3. **Show your work.** Every flag has a reason in words; accepting a false flag is one tap.
4. **Everything automatic is reversible.** Scratch, circle and undo; raw files are always kept.
5. **Honest states.** Pending looks pending; nothing claims coverage before its verdict.
6. **Designed for arm's length and a stand.** The creator stands 0.5–1.5 m away, so type sizes and targets suit that distance, and overrides work without touching the phone (volume keys, a Bluetooth selfie remote).
7. **Never color alone.** Covered, needed and pending each have an icon and a word; large type; a haptic option, tested so the vibration isn't audible in the recording.
8. **Mirrors well.** Capture and review stay legible mirrored to a laptop over Office Kit, so a second person (and the judges) can watch coverage live.

### 5.5 Questions for design review

- Strip placement relative to the iQOO 15's front camera (check the loaner's punch-hole position), and how much of the preview it may cover.
- How Safe to wrap feels: color, motion, and whether any sound plays (default off, since it would be recorded).
- The lined script's visual language: film lined scripts draw a vertical line per take down the lines it covers; adapt, don't copy.
- The one Tier 0 caption style: legible on phones, clear of Reels and Shorts UI overlays.
- On-set conditions: dark UI, glare, OLED burn-in on a long session.
- Name, icon and first-run screen.

### 6.1 The Coverage Engine (product definition, not API design)

- **Does:** given a script and a stream of audio, it tracks takes per line and emits coverage events and an edit list.
- **Doesn't:** own the camera, the UI, video files or the network.
- **Inputs:** a script (lines with stable ids and types); timestamped mono audio frames; a config (thresholds, pauses, command phrases, brand aliases); optional signals such as face presence.
- **Outputs:** events (take opened and closed, verdict with reasons, coverage changed, retake needed, take scratched, wrap ready), the append-only ledger, and the edit list.
- **Guarantees:** deterministic replay from a recorded transcript (same recognizer output, script, config and models give the same verdicts and the same ledger); no network access; stable line ids across sessions, including when a line's text is edited (R18); versioned config and model manifest. Determinism end to end from live audio is claimed only once it's measured that way (R14).

| Change a threshold on a loaner and see last session's verdicts change | No rebuild; under a minute per iteration |

| OEM engineer gets a first verdict from their own audio | Half a day, from a README and a sample |

**The magical moment for a developer:** drop in a WAV and a script, get the lined script and the edit list.

### 7.3 Evaluation

**Ground truth.** A person listens to each take against the script and labels it clean or flub. Each flub gets a miscue type borrowed from reading assessment (Microsoft Reading Progress): omission (skipped words), insertion (added words), mispronunciation (a misread or swapped word), repetition, and self-correction (including restarting the line). Reading assessment also supplies a per-take score: accuracy rate, the share of script words read correctly. On a normal line a small insertion can still be clean; on a must-say line it can't.

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| False-flag rate | Clean reads flagged ÷ all clean reads | The precision-first headline number |
| Verdict precision | Flagged takes that are real flubs ÷ all flagged takes | Trust in a prompt |
| Verdict recall | Real flubs flagged ÷ all real flubs | Coverage really is coverage |
| Reason accuracy | Flags whose reason matches the label ÷ all correct flags | "Show your work" is true |
| Safe-to-wrap correctness | Sessions where Safe to wrap appeared exactly when every line was truly covered ÷ sessions | The product promise in one number |
| Verdict latency | Verdict time minus end of speech; p50 and p90 against the line-end pause | Prompts land in the pause |
| Stop to first frame | Wrap tap to first frame of the cut | "No render bar" |

**Slices:** teammates vs strangers; quiet vs hall noise; short (5 words or fewer), medium and long lines; lines with numbers or brand names; two lines in one breath; spoken commands; **must-say lines against the same reads scored as normal lines**; takes where the face left the frame.

**Starting targets, reset from Friday's data:** false-flag rate at or below 5% on strangers; recall at or above 80%; p90 verdict latency below the line-end pause. The must-say slice carries its own false-flag target, because a word-perfect rule fails on any recognizer error that normalization doesn't absorb: at a 3% residual word error rate, a clean 15-word disclosure fails roughly a third of the time unless the rule is written to tolerate it. Must-say goes in front of a juror only if that slice is measured and clean (§1 Decisions).

**Held out.** Thresholds, the line-end pause, the alias list and every other config value are tuned on **teammates' reads only**. Every stranger read — Friday's and Saturday's volunteers — is held out: not looked at while tuning, and the only data the eval card reports. If a held-out number comes back bad, the fix is a config change re-run against the whole held-out set, and the card says how many times that happened. Numbers from tuning data are labeled as such and never go on a slide.

**Sample sizes, stated honestly.** Zero false flags in N clean reads bounds the true rate below about 3/N at 95% confidence (the rule of three). So 20 stranger clean reads with no flags proves only that the rate is under 15% — not under 5%. Claiming "under 5% on strangers" needs **60 or more held-out stranger clean reads** with none flagged. The target is therefore 60+ stranger clean reads (20+ on Friday, the rest from Saturday's volunteers) and 30+ labeled flubs for recall, all held out; teammates' reads are the tuning set and are counted separately. **If the count falls short, the card reports the bound the count supports** — "0 of 24 clean stranger reads flagged: under 12.5% at 95% confidence" — never the 5% target. The held-out set is re-run on a loaner Saturday against the shipped build.

**The card describes the demo build, or it says which build it describes.** Config is frozen when the held-out run starts, and any threshold changed afterwards invalidates the run: either re-run the held-out set on the new config (it's one command, §6.2) or print the build and config version on the card and say so. The overnight schedule makes this easy to get wrong — the eval runs 01:00–06:30 while 06:30–08:30 is still fixing and polishing — so the last held-out run happens **after** the final config change, on the frozen demo build, and it is the only one quoted.

**The eval card** is one slide and a README table: every metric above, per slice, with N, device and build named. The replay recognizer plus the corpus is the regression suite; every threshold change re-runs it. The card puts a naive baseline beside the aligner (the raw transcript compared with the script at one fixed word-error threshold), so it shows what the aligner earns, the way one earlier team showed rules against model on unseen cases.

## 10. Build Plan

Current proposal from v2, updated for v3 scope. `/plan-eng-review` specifies the contract and may re-cut this plan.

### Glossary

- **Project:** one script plus every session recorded against it. Line ids stay stable across sessions, so a pickup session joins the same cut.
- **Session:** one recording, from record to stop.
- **Line:** one item of the script, with a type (line, must-say; talking point from Tier 2; cutaway from Tier 3).
- **Take:** one spoken attempt at a line inside a session. A take can span several utterances.
- **Utterance:** a stretch of speech between two pauses, as cut by the voice detector.
- **Verdict:** the aligner's result for a closed take: clean, flubbed, or missing words, with a miscue reason.
- **Segment:** one entry in the edit list.

### The spine loop

```
 laptop ──Office Kit clipboard──▶ script ──────────────────┐
                                                            ▼
 mic ──▶ audio ──▶ voice detection ──▶ Recognizer ──▶ aligner ──▶ strip: "again, line N",
                                    (CPU | NPU | replay)   │        coverage, Safe to wrap
 front camera ──▶ video file ───────────┐                  ▼
                                        │          ledger (append-only)
 NPU vision model ──▶ face-in-frame chip│                  │
                                        ▼                  ▼
                             Wrap ──▶ edit list over raw files ──▶ instant playback
                                                            └────▶ background export ──▶ gallery or laptop
```

1. The creator starts a project by pasting the script from the laptop over the Office Kit clipboard: one line per row, optional line types.
2. A foreground service captures portrait 1080×1920 video from the front camera with CameraX, with the strip directly under the lens. Audio feeds recognition and the cut; how audio and video stay in sync is R1.
3. Voice detection cuts utterances at pauses. Each utterance goes to a `Recognizer` interface with three implementations: sherpa-onnx on the CPU (built first), an NPU recognizer (switched on by the Premise 7 bar), and a fake that replays text files for tests. An NPU vision model runs alongside for the face-in-frame signal (R9).
4. The aligner (rules below) opens and closes takes, applies scratches and appends verdicts to the ledger.
5. When a take closes as flubbed or missing words, the strip shows "again, line N" at that line-end pause; otherwise the line turns covered and the strip advances. When every line is covered, the strip shows Safe to wrap and Record becomes Wrap. Prompts never interrupt speech.
6. On stop, the ledger becomes the edit list. **Selection and ordering are separate rules (clarified Sep 11, R21).** *Selection:* per line, the circled take if there is one, else the latest clean take of that line across all of the project's sessions, by take-close time. *Ordering:* the cut is ordered by **script line order**, never by take-close time — otherwise a pickup of line 1 recorded last would play last. Close times are unique inside a session and a later session always wins, so "latest" never ties; text match, recognizer confidence and the off-frame signal are used to **rank takes when no take of a line is clean**, to choose between the halves of a one-breath split, and to pass over a clean take marked off-frame when another clean take of that line exists. Splices at voice-detector boundaries; scratched takes, command utterances, and takes whose video is missing are never included. Playback starts at once with line captions drawn from the script. Takes still awaiting a verdict play provisionally; late verdicts update the edit list before export. A line with no clean take uses its best-scoring take and is flagged; the review screen lists needed lines with a one-tap pickup session.
7. Export runs in a media-processing foreground service and never while recording: captions burned in (one style in Tier 0), audio merged in sync, edge fades applied. Raw files and the ledger are always kept.

### Aligner rules

- **Joining a take:** each utterance joins the open take, or opens a take for whichever line it matches best across **the whole script**, with a bias toward the current line, the next line and lines marked needed — a match to any other line has to beat those by a margin (start value in the runtime config). Covered lines are candidates too: re-reading a line that is already covered opens a new take on *that* line, not on the current one. Without this, a juror who redoes line 1 has the flub charged to line 3, which is a false flag on a clean read — the failure Premise 10 ranks worst. A retake of an earlier line after the strip has moved on still counts.
- **Two lines in one breath:** an utterance that matches the tail of line N and the head of line N+1 is split at a word boundary and covers both (R3). **A must-say line is never split** (new Sep 11): a split landing one word off fails the word-perfect rule on a clean read, which is Premise 10's worst failure on the disclosure line. Force the take to close at the must-say boundary instead.
- **Normalization:** script and transcript are both normalized before matching: case, punctuation, numbers, currency and dates (accepting "70k" and "seventy thousand rupees"), a per-project alias list for brand names, per-token fuzzy matching (R2).
- **Closing a take:** a take closes when silence passes the line-end pause, when the next utterance matches a different line, or on a tap or remote advance. Shorter pauses inside a line never close a take. **The start value is not the median between-line pause** — half of line ends would then fail to close on silence, and "again, line N" would arrive a line late. It's the value that best separates the two distributions Friday measures: above the long tail of within-line pauses, below the short tail of between-line pauses. Where they overlap the value leans short, and closing on "the next utterance matches a different line" catches the rest. Friday reports both distributions, not one median.
- **Verdicts:** clean means the line-length-aware similarity is at or above the clean threshold and no restart. Flubbed means a restart or similarity below the threshold. Missing words means the line's tail is absent when the take closes. Every verdict records its miscue reason (R4). Must-say lines use a strict threshold: every word, in order, allowing only normalization equivalents. Because that rule fails on any recognizer error normalization doesn't absorb, must-say has its own measured false-flag slice (§7.3) and its own decision about going in front of a juror (§1 Decisions); if the slice isn't clean, must-say stays in the wrap report and off the stage.
- **Scratches (The Vanish):** implicit: a later clean take of the same line supersedes earlier ones. Explicit: an utterance consisting only of a command phrase ("scratch that", "cut"; list in the runtime config) scratches the open take, or the most recent closed take if none is open, and is itself excluded from the edit list. Guard: the command utterance is short (start value under 1.5 s) and matches the command list better than any script line (R8). Tap and remote scratch emit the same event. Undo restores.
- **Filler splicing (new Sep 11, revising the v3.1 decision):** splicing applies to **flubbed takes only**. The clean-take splice ships behind a flag that is off on the demo build, and **must-say takes are never spliced** — brief mode would otherwise ship a legally-required disclosure the creator never said contiguously. Where the flag is on, splice only across a filler with more than 150 ms of silence on both sides; transducer token timestamps lag the acoustic event, so a tighter splice can cut mid-word.
- **Circles:** a circled take beats the latest-clean rule for its line.
- **Off-frame takes:** if the NPU vision signal says the face left the frame for more than a moment during a take, the take is marked off-frame. It is never flagged as a flub and never marks the line needed (precision first); it loses to any other clean take of that line in the edit list, and the review screen says why (R9).
- **Edited script lines:** editing a line's text returns that line to needed and marks its existing takes "read against an earlier version". The line id never changes, so pickups and earlier sessions still join the same cut (R18).
- **Lost video:** a take whose video didn't survive a crash is marked video missing. Its verdict stays in the ledger, but it can't cover a line and the cut skips it (R13).
- **Wrap ready:** every line covered, must-say lines by their strict verdict. **A pending verdict blocks wrap ready until it lands**, however long that takes — the hold limit moves the strip on, never the coverage state, so nothing claims coverage before its verdict (§5.4 principle 5). If a verdict never lands, the line stays pending and the wrap sheet says which line and why.
- **Pending verdicts:** the strip holds on the current line until the verdict arrives or a hold limit passes (start value 1.5 s). Past the limit it advances; a late flubbed or missing verdict marks the line needed and shows "again, line N" at the next line-end pause.
- **Skipped line:** if speech matches the next line better than the current one, the current line is marked needed and tracking moves on.
- **Ignored prompt:** nothing blocks. The line stays needed and shows in the pickup list.
- **If verdicts can't keep up** (R6 fails even on the NPU): live prompts switch off, the strip advances at every line-end pause, and needed lines collect in the pickup list, recorded before leaving the set.
- **Restart smoothing:** if Friday shows the recognizer turns restarts into clean text, flub detection falls back to repetition and duration cues (two takes matching the same line; a take far longer than the line's expected duration).
- **Tuning data:** teammates only. Strangers' reads — Friday's and Saturday's mentors or volunteers, recorded with permission — are held out and are the only data the eval card reports (§7.3), because the juror will be a stranger and the card has to survive being asked what it was tuned on.

### v3 → v3.1 (the Sep 11 read)

| Loophole found | v3 said | v3.1 says |
|---|---|---|
| Safe to wrap contradicted itself | A pending verdict holds wrap "until it lands or the hold limit passes" | A pending verdict blocks wrap until it lands. The hold limit moves the strip, never the coverage state |
| A re-read of a covered line was scored against the wrong line | Takes join the current line, the next line, or needed lines | Takes join whichever line they match best across the whole script, biased to those three. Covered lines are candidates |
| The eval card was graded on its own tuning data | Strangers and Saturday volunteers were both listed as tuning data | Tuning is teammates only; every stranger read is held out and is the only data the card reports |
| "≤5% false flags on strangers" from 20 reads | 20 stranger reads planned | 60+ held-out stranger clean reads, or the card reports the bound the count actually supports |
| Must-say had no test for the case that matters | Only "a missing word is flagged" | 20 consecutive clean must-say reads must draw zero flags, and that test decides whether it goes on stage |
| "It never left this phone", said while mirroring | Pitch line and beat sheet | "Nothing left this phone but the picture on that screen"; Office Kit is a row in the threat model |
| The prompt-timing test didn't test the prompt | p90 latency below the line-end pause | The prompt must be on screen before the reader starts the next line, 18 of 20; and the pause start value is no longer the median |
| The NPU recognition switch was latency-only | One bar: latency | Per requirement: R2, R3 and R8 must still pass with it; word timings gate the one-breath split |
| The NPU vision signal fed nothing, and paused when hot | Face chip; hot phone pauses tracking | Off-frame takes lose to other clean takes in the cut; hot phone lowers the duty cycle, never to zero |
| Nobody owned the pitch | "Pitch lead (unassigned)" | T1, with the deck rewritten Friday night |
| Tier 0 grew to 11 items with 3 Green hours | Ranking only | A cut list decided in advance: items 1–5 are Eval 1; 6–11 ranked, and anything not landed by 16:20 moves to the overnight block |
